Morning · Bruges

First train, there by 09:00 — Bruges before ten is a different city. Walk the Markt and the Burg, then the canal boat, thirty minutes, which is the correct way to see Bruges and takes a fraction of the time a museum would. The Begijnhof and the Minnewater on the way back to the station.

Afternoon · Brussels

Bruges to Brussels is an hour, direct. The Horta Museum house from the street in Saint-Gilles, the Old England building, the Grand Place, and Rue Antoine Dansaert for Belgian designers. Fifty minutes home.

What this costs youMerging the two means the Damme bike ride is gone — 7km of flat canal path out of Bruges that was the best afternoon of the Belgian week. If you'd rather have it back, split these again and drop Brussels; you'll see Horta's exteriors nowhere else, but you'll be on a bike instead.

The Leie boat from the Graslei, forty minutes — the only way to see how the guild houses meet the water. Then walk the Graslei and Korenlei and the outside of St Bavo's and Gravensteen; the castle looks better from the bridge than from inside anyway.

THU 17Rotterdam — water taxi and skyline›

Morning

Van Nelle Fabriek from the outside. Brinkman and Van der Vlugt, 1931 — the glass curtain wall and the elevated conveyor bridges read perfectly from the canal path and the approach road, and no tour is needed for the thing that actually matters. It's a working office park; walk the perimeter.

Afternoon

The water taxi across the Maas — fast yellow boats, a few euros, the best cheap thrill in the Netherlands. Then Depot Boijmans, whose mirrored bowl is the whole point and is free to look at, the Markthal (walk straight in, no ticket), the Cube Houses and the Erasmus Bridge on foot.

Evening

Black Smoke sits inside the Van Nelle Fabriek — so you get the interior after all, with brisket. From 17:00; eat at 17:30, 19:30 train, home by 20:45.

South: the chapel and Metz

Fri 18 – Sun 20 Sep · 2 nights, one bed · car

One field chapel, one Roman gate, one Shigeru Ban roof — and two nights in the same Metz bed instead of moving every night. You don’t come back to Antwerp: you carry on north to Utrecht, which is what frees a day for Efteling.

FRI 18Bruder Klaus, then straight to Metz›
2h45 + 1h45 + 1hlong driving day

Morning

Collect the car at 07:30. Antwerp to Wachendorf is 2h45 — the one genuinely long stretch of the trip, so coffee and a proper start.

Midday

Bruder Klaus Field Chapel. Park in the signposted lot at the edge of the village — there is no road to it — then walk 10 to 15 minutes up a gentle farm track through the fields. That walk is part of the building.

Zumthor poured rammed concrete in 24 layers around 112 tree trunks leaned together like a tipi, then burned the timber out. The interior is charred, ridged, and still smells of it. No windows: light comes through hundreds of small glass plugs and one open oculus, so rain falls onto the floor and stays there. Free, open 10:00–17:00, closed Mondays, season ends 1 October — directions.

It’s a working chapel and people come to pray. Twenty quiet minutes, then walk back down.

Message Emeline about parkingRue des Piques sits inside Metz’s pedestrianised cathedral quarter, and you arrive with a car you keep for six more days. Ask her before you travel: whether there’s a resident permit or a drop-off window for luggage, and which garage she recommends — Cathédrale and République are the two central underground ones. Worth sorting now rather than circling the old town at 17:30 with the bags.Low-emission stickerIf you route through Cologne, the city runs an Umweltzone and a Belgian-plated hire car needs the green sticker. Ask at the rental desk before you drive off.

Afternoon

South-west to Trier, 1h45 — the Porta Nigra is the largest Roman city gate north of the Alps and reads entirely from the square in front of it. An hour on your feet, outside, free, and it breaks the drive at the right point.

Metz is an hour on. Check-in at La Vue En Rose, 5 Rue des Piques, opens at 16:00 — so on this schedule you arrive comfortably after it, around 17:30.

Evening

Walk the Centre Pompidou-Metz parvis at dusk — Shigeru Ban’s woven hexagonal timber roof is lit from within and reads better from outside after dark than from inside in daylight. Then the old town and Place Saint-Louis.

SAT 19Waterfall, treetop walk, and back to Metz›
day loop · 3h20 drivingtreetop walk

Morning

Bags stay put. Saarburg, an hour north — a twenty-metre waterfall drops straight through the middle of the old town, which is as odd in person as it sounds. Walk up beside it, then coffee at the bottom.

Midday

The Saarschleife, 25 minutes further — the river doubles back on itself in a horseshoe, and a 1,250m treetop walkway runs out to a 42m tower above the bend. Step-free the whole way up, open daily until 31 October — treetop-walks.com. It’s an activity, not a viewpoint: give it two hours. Windproof layers, it’s exposed at the top.

Lunch in Mettlach or back down in Saarburg.

Afternoon

An hour and a quarter back to Metz. Nothing to check into, nothing to unpack.

Evening

Metz properly: the cathedral has more stained glass than any Gothic church in France, Chagall windows included, and you can walk in free. Then the Moselle islands and dinner in the old town.

SUN 20Metz to Utrecht›
4hwharf cellars

Morning

An unhurried start — the covered market in Metz for breakfast and something to eat in the car, then out.

Afternoon

Four hours north to Utrecht. You skip the Antwerp night that used to sit here — that’s what buys the Efteling day.

Evening

Utrecht is the surprise of this trip and nobody plans it. The Oudegracht runs a full storey below street level, with medieval wharf cellars opening straight onto the water and terraces along them — no other Dutch city has this. The Dom Tower stands detached from its church: the nave collapsed in a 1674 storm and was never rebuilt, so there’s a public square where the middle of the cathedral used to be. Free, outdoors, and the best building story in the country.

Eat on the wharf.

North: the Dutch run

Sun 20 – Thu 24 Sep · 4 nights · car · ends in Antwerp

Everything from your screenshots, plus a day at Efteling. Three nights in Utrecht without moving, then north to Groningen and across to Haarlem.

MON 21Castle, coloured houses, spheres›
car · 2h drivingbikes at Houten

Morning

Kasteel de Haar, 30 minutes out — the largest castle in the Netherlands, rebuilt 1892–1907 by Pierre Cuypers. Buy the gardens-only ticket: the park, the moats and the formal parterres are the better half, and the interior is normally shut in September anyway while the Van Zuylen family are in residence. Check the hours.

Midday

Rietplas, Houten, 30 minutes south. Gabled houses in flat primary colours standing straight out of the water. Walk the lake edge — and Houten is the most famously bike-planned town in the Netherlands, so if you want an hour on wheels, take it here.

Afternoon

Bolwoningen, ’s-Hertogenbosch, 30 minutes on. Fifty spherical concrete houses by Dries Kreijkamp, 1984 — the only estate of its kind anywhere. Lived-in social housing: you look from the pavement, take the photographs and go. Thirty minutes.

Then walk the old centre of Den Bosch and the St John’s Cathedral exterior — the best Gothic in the country, and the flying buttresses carry stone figures riding them like a procession.

Evening

Back to Utrecht, an hour. Early night — tomorrow starts at ten.

TUE 22Efteling›
50 min from UtrechtTuesday = cheapest + quietest10:00–18:00

Why Tuesday

You guessed right, and Tuesday beats Wednesday. Efteling runs dynamic pricing — the fare tracks expected crowds, so the quiet day and the cheap day are the same day. September sits outside every Dutch school holiday (autumn half-term isn’t until mid-October), which puts the whole week in the cheapest band.

Tuesday edges Wednesday for one local reason: Dutch primary schools finish at midday on Wednesdays, so Wednesday afternoons pick up families that Tuesday never sees. Crowd trackers put Tuesdays among the quietest days of the week in low season.

The day

Open 10:00–18:00 in September. Be at the gate for opening — the first two hours have the shortest queues of the day by a wide margin, whatever the season. Do Baron 1898, Symbolica and Danse Macabre first while everyone else is still buying coffee, then slow down.

Kaatsheuvel is 50 minutes from Utrecht by car. Parking is €15, paid separately.

Book online, and book earlyGate prices run €10–15 per person above online ones, and the dynamic fare climbs as the date fills. Buying now, for a Tuesday in September, is the cheapest this ticket will ever be — expect the low end of the range rather than the peak. Book direct at efteling.com.

Evening

Back to Utrecht for the third night. Nothing to pack, nothing to move.

WED 23Pancakes, Giethoorn, Groningen›
45m + 1h15 + 1h30whisper boatsunset 19:30

Late morning

Hans & Grietje, Zeewolde — 45 minutes north, on the edge of the Harderbos. Pancakes inside a crooked witch's house where the tables turn, the floors tilt and the ceiling is bewitched, built new in 2016 by two former dairy farmers. Book a table and go before 11:00; it's a family play park and it fills.

Midday

Giethoorn, 1h15 on. A village with no roads — just canals, footbridges and thatched farmhouses on islands. Hire your own whisper boat (silent electric, no licence needed) for two hours and drive yourselves; the guided barges go where everyone else goes. This is the single best outdoor activity in the Netherlands and it replaces Breklenkamp, which was a farmhouse in a field with nothing to do.

Evening

Groningen, 1h30 north. Straight to Reitdiephaven for sunset, about 19:30 — tall narrow gabled houses in deep reds, oranges and blues along a marina, doubled exactly in still water. That hour is the entire reason this place is on your list.

Tonight · GroningenCentral, entire place, target €140. A student city — good value, lively midweek.

THU 24Windmills, then back to Antwerp›
2h + 2h30last driving day

Morning

Away by 08:30. Two hours south-west to Zaanse Schans — working windmills along the river, green timber houses, cheese and clog workshops. The village is free and open; a couple of mills charge two or three euros to climb inside, and the one grinding pigment is the one to pick. You’ll be there by 10:30, well before the coaches.

Afternoon

Two and a half hours south to Antwerp. Drop the car where you picked it up — same city, so no cross-border drop fee, which is worth about €200 on its own.

You’re back by three with nothing left to drive and nothing left to organise.

Evening

Dinner with your uncle. Ten nights of his flat is the reason this trip fit the budget at all, and arriving back with a boot full of Alsace wine and Zaanse cheese is the right way to say it. Ciro’s or Le Steak ’17 if you’d rather go out.

Amsterdam by day

Fri 25 – Sat 26 Sep · one night in Haarlem

One last hop north after the goodbyes. Two half-days in Amsterdam and a Haarlem market morning, then Schiphol — thirty minutes away.

FRI 25 – SAT 26North again: Amsterdam, then home›
Antwerp → Haarlem 2h15cruise 19:00EK150 dep 22:00

Friday morning · the last move

Goodbyes, then the direct train north. Antwerp to Haarlem is about 2h15 via Amsterdam — one change, and you’re dropping bags in the Jordaan’s quieter cousin by midday.

Friday afternoon · Amsterdam, fifteen minutes away

Straight in on the train. Van Stapele first — one warm chocolate cookie, it’s all they make, and they sell out. Then the Nine Streets and the Jordaan canals on foot, Fabel Friet and Miuz Gelato when you need them.

Then hire bikes and ride Vondelpark end to end, past the Rijksmuseum — the cycle passage straight through the middle of the building is public and free, and it’s the best thing about it — and the Van Gogh and Stedelijk exteriors across the lawn.

Friday evening · the water

Early dinner at Cannibale Royale, then a sunset canal cruise at 19:00 — sunset is about 19:20, so ninety minutes covers all of it. Small open boat, not a glass-top: twelve to twenty people, a skipper who talks, your own drinks, and the narrow canals the big boats can’t enter. Board on the Prinsengracht. Trains back to Haarlem run four an hour, late.

Saturday morning · Haarlem’s own market

Saturday is market day on the Grote Markt under the Grote Kerk, four minutes from your door — flowers, cheese, bread, almost entirely locals. Walk the hofjes afterwards: small almshouse courtyards you push a door and walk into, free, scattered all over the old town.

Saturday afternoon · one more, or none

Back into Amsterdam for the free IJ ferry behind Centraal — five minutes, no ticket — to the Eye Filmmuseum terrace with the city across the water. Or Het Schip in the Spaarndammerbuurt, de Klerk’s 1919 workers’ housing, which reads completely from the street. Or the Noordermarkt flea market and Winkel 43’s apple pie on the same square.

Or none of it, and a last walk along the Spaarne.

Saturday evening

Collect the bags. Haarlem to Schiphol is 30 minutes direct, several an hour. Check-in opens 18:30, EK150 departs 22:00.
