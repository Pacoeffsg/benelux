South: the chapel and Metz

Fri 18 – Sun 20 Sep · 2 nights, one bed · car

One field chapel, one Roman gate, one Shigeru Ban roof — and two nights in the same Metz bed instead of moving every night. You don’t come back to Antwerp: you carry on north to Utrecht, which is what frees a day for Efteling.

FRI 18Bruder Klaus, then straight to Metz›
2h45 + 1h45 + 1hlong driving day

Morning

Collect the car at 07:30. Antwerp to Wachendorf is 2h45 — the one genuinely long stretch of the trip, so coffee and a proper start.

Midday

Bruder Klaus Field Chapel.IG Park in the signposted lot at the edge of the village — there is no road to it — then walk 10 to 15 minutes up a gentle farm track through the fields. That walk is part of the building.

Zumthor poured rammed concrete in 24 layers around 112 tree trunks leaned together like a tipi, then burned the timber out. The interior is charred, ridged, and still smells of it. No windows: light comes through hundreds of small glass plugs and one open oculus, so rain falls onto the floor and stays there. Free, open 10:00–17:00, closed Mondays, season ends 1 October — directions.

It’s a working chapel and people come to pray. Twenty quiet minutes, then walk back down.

Low-emission stickerIf you route through Cologne, the city runs an Umweltzone and a Belgian-plated hire car needs the green sticker. Ask at the rental desk before you drive off.

Afternoon

South-west to TrierIG, 1h45 — the Porta Nigra is the largest Roman city gate north of the Alps and reads entirely from the square in front of it. An hour on your feet, outside, free, and it breaks the drive at the right point.

Metz is an hour on. Check-in at La Vue En Rose, 5 Rue des Piques, opens at 16:00 — so on this schedule you arrive comfortably after it, around 17:30.

Evening

Walk the Centre Pompidou-MetzIG parvis at dusk — Shigeru Ban’s woven hexagonal timber roof is lit from within and reads better from outside after dark than from inside in daylight. Then the old town and Place Saint-Louis.

Bags stay put. SaarburgIG, an hour north — a twenty-metre waterfall drops straight through the middle of the old town, which is as odd in person as it sounds. Walk up beside it, then coffee at the bottom.

Step-free the whole way up, open daily until 31 October — treetop-walks.com. It’s an activity, not a viewpoint: give it two hours. Windproof layers, it’s exposed at the top.

Lunch in Mettlach or back down in Saarburg.

An hour and a quarter back to Metz. Nothing to check into, nothing to unpack.

An unhurried start — the covered market in Metz for breakfast and something to eat in the car, then out.

Four hours north to Utrecht. You skip the Antwerp night that used to sit here — that’s what buys the Efteling day.

Free, outdoors, and the best building story in the country.

North: the Dutch run

Sun 20 – Thu 24 Sep · 4 nights · car · ends in Antwerp

Everything from your screenshots, plus a day at Efteling. Three nights in Utrecht without moving, then north to Groningen and back down to Antwerp.

MON 21Spheres, coloured houses, then the castle›
car · 2h10 drivingcastle 14:30 · bookedsouth, then north

09:00 · collect the car

A relaxed start after yesterday’s four hours. The day runs south first, then back up — one clean arc instead of a zigzag past Utrecht twice.

10:30 · ’s-Hertogenbosch

BolwoningenIG — fifty spherical concrete houses by Dries Kreijkamp, 1984, the only estate of its kind anywhere. Lived-in social housing: you look from the pavement, take the photographs and go. Thirty minutes, and morning light on white domes beats midday glare.

Then the old centre: St John’s CathedralIG from the square — the flying buttresses carry stone figures riding them like a procession — and the market place. Lunch here, and the Bossche bol is the town’s own: a fist-sized chocolate-covered cream pastry.

13:30 · Houten

Rietplas, HoutenIG — gabled houses in flat primary colours standing straight out of the water. Walk the lake edge; half an hour is right. Houten is the most famously bike-planned town in the Netherlands if you want twenty minutes on wheels.

14:30 · Kasteel de Haar

Kasteel de HaarIG — the largest castle in the Netherlands, rebuilt 1892–1907 by Pierre Cuypers, the architect of the Rijksmuseum and Amsterdam Centraal. Moats, turrets, formal parterres, and central heating hidden behind a medieval silhouette.

Arrive by 14:10. The car park sits away from the castle and you walk in through the grounds. Interior first on the timed slot, then the gardens with the light behind them — and since nothing follows, this is the one thing today that can run long.

Twenty-five minutes. Early dinner on the wharf — Efteling starts at ten tomorrow.

TUE 22Efteling›
50 min from UtrechtTuesday = cheapest + quietest10:00–18:00

Why Tuesday

You guessed right, and Tuesday beats Wednesday. Efteling runs dynamic pricing — the fare tracks expected crowds, so the quiet day and the cheap day are the same day. September sits outside every Dutch school holiday (autumn half-term isn’t until mid-October), which puts the whole week in the cheapest band.

Tuesday edges Wednesday for one local reason: Dutch primary schools finish at midday on Wednesdays, so Wednesday afternoons pick up families that Tuesday never sees. Crowd trackers put Tuesdays among the quietest days of the week in low season.

The day

Open 10:00–18:00 in September. Be at the gate for opening — the first two hours have the shortest queues of the day by a wide margin, whatever the season. Do Baron 1898IG, SymbolicaIG and Danse MacabreIG first while everyone else is still buying coffee, then slow down.

Kaatsheuvel is 50 minutes from Utrecht by car. Parking is €15, paid separately.

Book online, and book earlyGate prices run €10–15 per person above online ones, and the dynamic fare climbs as the date fills. Buying now, for a Tuesday in September, is the cheapest this ticket will ever be — expect the low end of the range rather than the peak. Book direct at efteling.com.

Evening

Back to Utrecht for the third night. Nothing to pack, nothing to move.

Late morning

Hans & Grietje, ZeewoldeIG — 45 minutes north, on the edge of the Harderbos. Pancakes inside a crooked witch's house where the tables turn, the floors tilt and the ceiling is bewitched, built new in 2016 by two former dairy farmers. Book a table and go before 11:00; it's a family play park and it fills.

Midday

GiethoornIG, 1h15 on. A village with no roads — just canals, footbridges and thatched farmhouses on islands. Hire your own whisper boat (silent electric, no licence needed) for two hours and drive yourselves; the guided barges go where everyone else goes. This is the single best outdoor activity in the Netherlands and it replaces Breklenkamp, which was a farmhouse in a field with nothing to do.

Evening

Groningen, 1h30 north. Straight to ReitdiephavenIG for sunset, about 19:30 — tall narrow gabled houses in deep reds, oranges and blues along a marina, doubled exactly in still water. That hour is the entire reason this place is on your list.

Away by 08:30. Two hours south-west to Zaanse SchansIG — working windmills along the river, green timber houses, cheese and clog workshops. Amsterdam by day

Fri 25 – Sat 26 Sep · one night in Noord

One last hop north after the goodbyes. Two half-days in the centre and a Noord morning — De Ceuvel, the Eye, NDSM — then Schiphol via Zuid.

FRI 25 – SAT 26North again: Amsterdam, then home›
Antwerp → Amsterdam 1h45cruise 19:00EK150 dep 22:00

Friday morning · the last move

Goodbyes, then the direct train north — Antwerp to Amsterdam Centraal is about 1h45, no change. From Centraal it’s one stop on metro 52 to Noorderpark, then a 20-minute walk. Drop the bags and come straight back in; treat the flat as somewhere you sleep, not a base.

Friday afternoon · Amsterdam, fifteen minutes away

Straight in on the train. Van StapeleIG first — one warm chocolate cookie, it’s all they make, and they sell out. Then the Nine StreetsIG and the Jordaan canals on foot, Fabel FrietIG and Miuz GelatoIG when you need them.

Then hire bikes and ride VondelparkIG end to end, past the RijksmuseumIG — the cycle passage straight through the middle of the building is public and free, and it’s the best thing about it — and the Van GoghIG and StedelijkIG exteriors across the lawn.

Cut east through the old centre on the way: Dam SquareIG and the Royal PalaceIG facade — a 17th-century town hall standing on 13,000 timber piles — then the Oude KerkIG, the oldest building in the city, and out via the BloemenmarktIG floating flower stalls and the Begijnhof courtyard.

Friday evening · the water

Early dinner at Cannibale RoyaleIG, then a sunset canal cruise at 19:00 — sunset is about 19:20, so ninety minutes covers all of it. Small open boat, not a glass-top: twelve to twenty people, a skipper who talks, your own drinks, and the narrow canals the big boats can’t enter. Board on the Prinsengracht. Getting home: metro 52 from Centraal runs until about half past midnight. After a late cruise and a fortnight on your feet, a taxi from Centraal is around €20 and worth it once.

Saturday morning · Noord, on your doorstep

Breakfast at Papillon BakeryIG, a few minutes from the flat.

Then De CeuvelIG — a former shipyard turned into a cluster of salvaged houseboats set on polluted ground that is being cleaned by the plants growing through it. Café, workspaces, a jetty. One of the more genuinely interesting things built in Amsterdam this century, and it is on your doorstep rather than a detour.

Walk down to the water for Eye FilmmuseumIG — you are on its side of the IJ now — and the terrace looking back at the city. NDSM wharfIG is the other direction: cranes, street art and the old shipbuilding sheds.

Saturday afternoon · one more, or none

Back into Amsterdam for the free IJ ferryIG behind Centraal — five minutes, no ticket — to the Eye FilmmuseumIG terrace with the city across the water. Or Het SchipIG in the Spaarndammerbuurt, de Klerk’s 1919 workers’ housing, which reads completely from the street. Or the NoordermarktIG flea market and Winkel 43IG’s apple pie on the same square.

Or none of it, and a last hour by the water in Noord.

Saturday evening

Collect the bags and leave Noord by 16:30. Walk to Noorderpark, metro 52 south to Amsterdam Zuid (about 12 minutes, through Centraal), then 7 minutes on the train to Schiphol. Allow the full hour.

Two legs, two ticketsNo metro reaches Schiphol — the airport is mainline only, so there is always a change. Metro and train are separate systems: tap a contactless card on and off for each leg. Zuid is far calmer than Centraal with luggage, but Centraal has more trains if anything goes wrong. Check metro 52 is running that morning; weekend engineering closures happen.
